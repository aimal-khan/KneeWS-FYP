TL;DR: Two of the three proposed additions fit KneeWS cleanly. The third should be constrained. I recommend adding multi-format MRI input and a structured radiology-style AI analysis report. For specialist recommendations, the defensible version is specialist-type/referral guidance, not recommending a specific doctor or giving treatment advice based solely on model probabilities.

The existing research design already supports image-only inference producing probabilities for all 12 abnormalities. deep-research-report

Feasibility of the proposed features
1. MRI → radiology-style report + probabilities for all 12 abnormalities

Feasibility: High, with one important modification.

The probability part is already fundamental to KneeWS:

MRI → model → probability for each of the 12 abnormalities.

The research report explicitly defines the final output as a 12-dimensional probability vector. RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection…

Generating a structured radiology-style analysis report from those results is also straightforward. For example, the system can organize its output into sections such as:

Study summary

Predicted findings

Abnormalities with high/moderate probability

Confidence values

Relevant MRI evidence

Limitations / uncertainty

However, I would not register the feature as “generate a radiologist's report” without qualification.

A real radiology report may describe:

exact anatomical location;

morphology;

severity;

tear grade;

measurements;

postoperative changes;

incidental findings;

whereas our model is fundamentally trained to predict 12 study-level abnormalities, not all of those details. RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection…

So the correct feature is:

AI-Generated Structured MRI Analysis Report — Generates a radiology-style structured summary of the model's predicted abnormalities, probabilities, uncertainty, and available model evidence.

This is useful and does not create a second major research problem.

2. Support multiple MRI formats

Feasibility: High, and it fits naturally.

The RSNA dataset itself is DICOM-based, and the current architecture already requires reconstruction of multiple DICOM series and slices. deep-research-report

Supporting additional MRI formats can be implemented through an input normalization layer:

Different MRI formats
        ↓
Format-specific reader
        ↓
Common internal study representation
        ↓
MRI preprocessing
        ↓
Model

The safest committed scope is:

DICOM / DICOM series — primary and mandatory.

NIfTI (.nii, .nii.gz) — reasonable secondary MRI format.

Packaged DICOM study/folder upload — useful system-level capability.

I would not promise generic JPEG/PNG MRI analysis at registration. Those formats can lose volumetric structure and important DICOM metadata, while KneeWS is designed around a multi-series MRI examination rather than arbitrary individual images. The research report emphasizes that each examination can contain varying numbers of series and slices, orientations, and acquisition characteristics. RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection…

So call the feature:

Multi-Format MRI Ingestion and Standardization

rather than simply “supports all image formats.”

The exact supported formats can be finalized after the dataset/POC audit.

3. Doctor/specialist recommendations according to disease probabilities

Feasibility: technically easy, but this needs careful scoping.

There are two possible interpretations.

A. Recommend a type of specialist

For example:

High probability of ligament/meniscal abnormality
→ Orthopedic / sports-medicine specialist review suggested

This is technically straightforward.

It can be implemented using a carefully defined mapping from finding categories to specialist categories, with uncertainty thresholds and explicit wording that this is referral guidance rather than medical diagnosis.

I consider this feasible as a supporting product feature.

B. Recommend specific doctors

For example:

“Visit Dr. XYZ at Hospital ABC.”

I would not currently include this.

That would require additional information unrelated to the research problem:

user's location;

doctor directory;

specialty credentials;

availability;

potentially insurance/network;

ranking criteria;

external APIs/data maintenance.

That begins turning KneeWS into a healthcare marketplace/referral application and weakens system coherence.

So unless you specifically mean named doctors, I recommend the feature:

Specialist Referral Guidance — Based on the pattern and confidence of detected abnormalities, the system identifies an appropriate specialist category for further professional review while clearly communicating that the recommendation is not a diagnosis or treatment decision.

The research report itself cautions that competition performance does not establish clinical readiness or downstream treatment effectiveness. RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection…

Revised Project Scope

I would now use this version in the submission form:

KneeWS is a research-oriented medical-imaging system that investigates how multilingual radiology reports can be used as reliable weak supervision for training multi-label knee MRI models when expert-annotated labels are scarce. Instead of treating report-derived labels as perfect binary ground truth, the system will distinguish positive, explicitly negative, uncertain, and unmentioned findings, estimate the reliability of these report states using available expert-labeled cases, and generate noise-aware probabilistic training targets. These targets will be used to train and evaluate an image-based model for twelve knee abnormalities from heterogeneous multi-series MRI examinations. The completed system will support standardized ingestion of multiple MRI formats, perform image-only inference, present the probability of each supported abnormality, generate a structured radiology-style AI analysis report, provide model evidence and uncertainty for review, and offer specialist-category referral guidance where appropriate. It will additionally provide controlled comparison between conventional hard pseudo-label supervision and the proposed noise-aware approach, together with disagreement analysis and research evaluation. There are significant challenges

You should understand these before committing it as a registration feature.

1. Report generation is much harder than classification

Classification asks:

Is ACL abnormal?

Report generation asks:

What structures are abnormal, how are they abnormal, where are they abnormal, how severe are the findings, and how should this be expressed medically?

That is a much richer output space.

2. The training reports themselves are noisy

This is especially relevant to KneeWS.

Our entire research hypothesis says:

The clinical report isn't necessarily perfect ground truth.

But now we would train another model to imitate those reports.

So:

generated report

would represent:

“a model-generated approximation of the original clinical reporting style”

rather than:

“the definitive truth about the MRI.”

This distinction is essential.

3. Hallucination becomes a serious problem

A text-generating model can generate findings unsupported by the MRI.

For instance:

“ACL tear present”

when the model does not actually have strong evidence.

For a medical system this needs to be explicitly evaluated.

4. Evaluation is difficult

For classification:

Prediction = ACL tear
Ground truth = ACL tear

Comparison is straightforward.

For reports:

Reference

“Moderate joint effusion.”

Generated

“A moderate amount of fluid is present within the knee joint.”

Those are semantically almost identical but textually different.

So BLEU/ROUGE alone are weak.

You would need some combination of:

clinical finding extraction;

semantic similarity;

consistency with the 12 labels;

hallucination/error analysis;

potentially expert/proxy evaluation.

That adds substantial experimental work.

Project Scope

KneeWS is a research-oriented medical-imaging system that investigates how multilingual radiology reports can be used as reliable weak supervision for training multi-label knee MRI models when expert-annotated labels are scarce. Instead of treating report-derived labels as perfect binary ground truth, the system will distinguish positive, explicitly negative, uncertain, and unmentioned findings, estimate the reliability of these states using available expert-labeled cases, and generate noise-aware probabilistic training targets. These targets will be used to train and evaluate a multi-series MRI model for twelve knee abnormalities. The completed system will support multiple medical-imaging input formats with DICOM as the primary format, perform image-only analysis of new knee MRI studies, provide probabilities for all twelve supported abnormalities, generate a structured radiology-style findings report from the learned MRI representation, present prediction confidence and model evidence for review, and provide specialist-category referral guidance where appropriate. It will also support controlled comparison between conventional hard pseudo-label supervision and the proposed noise-aware approach, together with disagreement and failure analysis. KneeWS is intended as a medical-imaging research and decision-support system and not as a clinically validated diagnostic or treatment system. deep-research-report
