# RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection as a Research Foundation for an Undergraduate FYP\\\
\\\
**Research cutoff:** August 24, 2026. The RSNA/Kaggle competition is still active, so participant methods, leaderboard positions, and even some competition documentation may change before the October 22, 2026 deadline. Statements below distinguish **official fact**, **public-file observation**, **research inference**, and **proposed hypothesis** wherever that distinction matters. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
## Executive assessment and challenge definition\\\
\\\
**Executive Summary**\\\
\\\
**Yes \\\\u2014 the RSNA 2026 Knee Abnormality Detection dataset is a strong foundation for a research-oriented FYP, but only if you do *not* frame the project as \\\\u201cbuild the best knee abnormality classifier.\\\\u201d**\\\
\\\
The scientifically interesting part of this challenge is not that it contains knee MRIs, nor that there are 12 abnormalities. Both single-pathology and multi-pathology knee-MRI classification are already fairly mature. Recent multicenter work has trained systems for nine knee abnormalities on more than 12,000 patients, and a 2026 study evaluated 23 manually labeled knee conditions using routine multisequence MRI. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search2\\\\ue202turn42search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
What makes the 2026 RSNA data unusually research-rich is the **combination** of:\\\
\\\
- thousands of real clinical knee MRI examinations;\\\
- variable numbers of DICOM series and slices;\\\
- multiple imaging planes and MRI sequence types;\\\
- twelve simultaneous study-level targets;\\\
- international, multicenter acquisition;\\\
- original free-text radiology reports in roughly a dozen languages;\\\
- reports available as supervision during training but apparently **not at competition inference time**;\\\
- only a very small public subset with direct expert image-review labels;\\\
- a hidden evaluation reference set independently annotated by musculoskeletal radiologists;\\\
- unavoidable semantic mismatch between what the original clinical report says and what later challenge readers label from the images. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue202turn36search1\\\\ue202turn36search2\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
That turns the problem into something more interesting than ordinary supervised classification:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{MRI study} + \\\\\\\\text{noisy multilingual report supervision} + \\\\\\\\text{few strong labels}\\\
\\\\\\\\longrightarrow\\\
12\\\\\\\\text{-label image-only prediction}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
In research terms, it is simultaneously a problem in **weak supervision, learning with noisy labels, multimodal learning with privileged information, multi-instance MRI representation, multilingual clinical NLP, and potentially domain generalization**.\\\
\\\
My strongest preliminary recommendation is therefore:\\\
\\\
\u003e **Study how naturally occurring multilingual radiology reports should be converted into or used as supervision when they are not themselves the reference standard.**\\\
\\\
The most promising three FYP directions are:\\\
\\\
**First:** noise-aware weak supervision from multilingual reports, particularly distinguishing **positive / explicitly negative / uncertain / unmentioned** findings rather than naively treating every non-mention as negative.\\\
\\\
**Second:** treating reports as **privileged training information** and experimentally comparing hard pseudo-labeling against image\\\\u2013text representation learning and teacher\\\\u2013student distillation.\\\
\\\
**Third:** robust modeling of a knee MRI examination as a **variable set of series**, with explicit testing of missing-series and protocol robustness rather than assuming every examination has the same three canonical inputs.\\\
\\\
The first is my preferred FYP because it has the best balance of novelty, clean experimental hypotheses, modest compute, dataset specificity, and useful negative results.\\\
\\\
**What the RSNA 2026 Challenge Actually Is**\\\
\\\
RSNA describes the challenge as developing models to \\\\u201cdetect and classify knee abnormalities.\\\\u201d It is RSNA's first challenge focused on musculoskeletal MRI and its first challenge to use medical images together with multilingual radiology-report text. RSNA says the overall dataset contains more than 5,000 knee MRI examinations, reports in roughly a dozen languages, and data from international sites. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The Kaggle task is more precise: competitors predict **12 clinically important abnormalities** from each knee MRI study. The submission columns are:\\\
\\\
`ACL`, `MCL`, `Medial Meniscus`, `Lateral Meniscus`, `Medial OA`, `Lateral OA`, `PF OA`, `Effusion`, `Synovitis`, `Baker's`, `Contusion`, and `Fracture`. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn16search4\\\\ue202turn9search0\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Here:\\\
\\\
- **ACL** refers to the anterior cruciate ligament.\\\
- **MCL** refers to the medial collateral ligament.\\\
- The two meniscal labels concern the medial and lateral menisci.\\\
- **Medial OA**, **Lateral OA**, and **PF OA** represent osteoarthritic abnormalities in the medial, lateral, and patellofemoral compartments.\\\
- **Effusion** is excess joint fluid.\\\
- **Synovitis** is inflammation of synovial tissue.\\\
- A **Baker cyst** is a popliteal fluid collection associated with the knee joint.\\\
- **Contusion** concerns traumatic bone/marrow injury.\\\
- **Fracture** concerns osseous fracture.\\\
\\\
The output is therefore **not** a segmentation, bounding-box detection, or localization task despite the word \\\\u201cDetection\\\\u201d in the challenge name. The competition submission target is a vector of 12 probabilities for each **StudyInstanceUID**, so mathematically it is **study-level multi-label binary classification**. Multiple conditions may coexist in one examination. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn9search0\\\\ue202turn16search4\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The metric is the unweighted mean of the ROC AUC for each of the 12 labels:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\mathrm{Score} =\\\
\\\\\\\\frac{1}{12}\\\\\\\\sum_{k=1}^{12}\\\\\\\\mathrm{AUC}_k .\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
Thus every finding contributes equally to the final competition score regardless of prevalence. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn2search0\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The public leaderboard is computed on approximately 30% of the hidden test set, meaning leaderboard optimization is particularly unsuitable as the primary scientific methodology for an FYP. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn0search10\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The competition is also a Kaggle **code competition** with a restricted execution environment; public rules describe a nine-hour notebook budget and disabled internet for submitted inference notebooks. RSNA has introduced separate efficiency awards in this challenge. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn14search0\\\\ue202turn14search2\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Clinical Problem**\\\
\\\
A radiologist does not normally inspect a single knee image. A knee MRI examination contains multiple sequences and planes designed to expose different tissue properties. The reader integrates evidence across the ACL and other ligaments, menisci, cartilage, bone marrow, joint fluid, synovium, and surrounding soft tissues.\\\
\\\
RSNA's clinical rationale is that MRI provides a whole-joint assessment of internal knee derangement and abnormalities involving ligaments, menisci, cartilage, marrow, effusion, synovium and cysts, while interpretation varies across readers and access to specialty-trained musculoskeletal radiologists is uneven. The intended clinical role is therefore closer to **consistent decision support or triage for a complex examination** than to a standalone single-lesion detector. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
This choice of knee MRI makes sense clinically because the examination has high information density: different pathologies can coexist and may be visible primarily on different planes or pulse sequences. The challenge consequently tests whether an AI system can integrate an examination rather than classify one canonical image.\\\
\\\
It would nevertheless be inappropriate to claim from the competition alone that an algorithm is ready for clinical diagnosis. The competition does not measure prospective workflow outcomes, downstream treatment decisions, calibration under real prevalence, or interaction with radiologists. Recent knee-AI studies that specifically examined radiologist assistance illustrate that these are separate questions from standalone AUC. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search2\\\\ue202turn40search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Machine-Learning Problem**\\\
\\\
A useful formalization is:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
X_i=\\\\\\\\{S_{i1}, S_{i2},...,S_{im_i}\\\\\\\\}\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
where \\\\\\\\(X_i\\\\\\\\) is one knee MRI study and \\\\\\\\(S_{ij}\\\\\\\\) is one DICOM series. Each series contains a variable sequence of slices.\\\
\\\
The target is\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
Y_i \\\\\\\\in \\\\\\\\{0,1\\\\\\\\}^{12}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
The model ultimately produces\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
f(X_i)\\\\\\\\rightarrow [0,1]^{12}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
The complication is that for most public training examples, \\\\\\\\(Y_i\\\\\\\\) is **not directly supplied as expert image-review ground truth**. Instead, a free-text clinical report \\\\\\\\(R_i\\\\\\\\) is available. Public Kaggle inspection consistently reports 4,407 training studies but only **58 studies with official/expert labels**, leaving the reports as the practical supervision source for the overwhelming majority of training cases. Competition-host discussion also confirms the unusual annotation setup. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search14\\\\ue202turn30search17\\\\ue202turn36search2\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
So the actual learning problem is more accurately represented as:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
(X_i,R_i,\\\\\\\\tilde{Y}_i)\\\
\\\\\\\\quad\\\\\\\\text{for most training studies}\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
where \\\\\\\\(\\\\\\\\tilde{Y}_i\\\\\\\\) is a weak label inferred from \\\\\\\\(R_i\\\\\\\\), and\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
(X_i,R_i,Y_i)\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
for the small expert-labeled subset.\\\
\\\
At inference, public inspection of `test.csv` shows only a study identifier and no `Report` column. Public notebooks explicitly note that **reports are train-only**. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search20\\\\ue202turn36search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
That distinction is extremely important. It means a model that needs a radiology report at test time cannot solve the Kaggle task. Reports are better understood as **privileged information available during training**.\\\
\\\
There is a minor documentation ambiguity worth recording. RSNA's launch article says the challenge combines images and report text to \\\\u201ctrain and test AI models,\\\\u201d whereas the current dedicated RSNA page describes the reports as being used to **train** AI models, and public Kaggle file inspection shows no test report column. The operational Kaggle setup therefore appears to be training-time report use with image-only inference. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue202turn36search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
This is scientifically much more interesting than conventional supervised classification.\\\

## Dataset anatomy, labels, reports, multilingual complexity, and risks\\\
\\\
**Detailed Dataset Anatomy**\\\
\\\
There are several important numerical discrepancies between official RSNA descriptions and the released Kaggle training files.\\\
\\\
RSNA's current challenge page says the dataset contains **more than 5,000 knee MRI exams from 19 sites**. The August 5 launch story instead says the **training dataset** contains more than 5,000 exams from **16 sites**. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Public Kaggle notebook inspection, meanwhile, consistently reports:\\\
\\\
- **4,407 rows** in `train.csv`;\\\
- **24,371 training series**;\\\
- approximately **820,000 DICOM slices** according to one detailed participant pipeline;\\\
- only **58 officially labeled training studies**. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue202turn30search14\\\\ue202turn30search17\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Those counts imply, for the public training set, approximately:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
24,371 / 4,407 \\\\\\\\approx 5.53\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
series per examination on average, and the participant's approximate 820,000-slice count would correspond to roughly 186 images per study and 34 per series. These are **derived from public participant inspection, not figures I independently verified from downloaded competition archives**. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The likely explanation for the \\\\u201c\u003e5,000\\\\u201d versus \\\\u201c4,407\\\\u201d discrepancy is that RSNA's headline number may describe a broader challenge corpus including hidden assessment cases, while the public Kaggle count describes only released training studies. However, because the launch story explicitly calls its \u003e5,000 figure the \\\\u201ctraining dataset,\\\\u201d that reconciliation remains an **inference rather than a verified fact**. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue202turn30search14\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The site count has a similar evolution. The dedicated RSNA page currently says **19 sites**, while the launch article says 16. RSNA's acknowledgments identify 19 principal data-contributing institutions and then separately list three \\\\u201cadditional contributing sites.\\\\u201d The main 19 institutions span 15 named countries by a straightforward count of the acknowledgment list. This makes 19 the most defensible **current core-site figure**, but it remains unclear whether some or all of the three additional sites contributed exams actually included in the final released challenge partitions. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn44view0\\\\ue202turn44view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Among the principal data contributors named by RSNA are institutions in Belgium, Argentina, Thailand, Taiwan, Morocco, Chile, T\\\\u00fcrkiye, Malta, Canada, Bulgaria, the United States, Croatia, Greece, Germany, and Bosnia and Herzegovina. Additional acknowledged sites are in Mongolia, Australia, and Italy. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn44view0\\\\ue202turn44view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
That is an exceptionally broad geographic footprint for a publicly accessible knee-MRI machine-learning challenge.\\\
\\\
**MRI organization.** The natural hierarchy is:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{Study / exam}\\\
\\\\\\\\rightarrow\\\
\\\\\\\\text{MRI series}\\\
\\\\\\\\rightarrow\\\
\\\\\\\\text{DICOM instances / slices}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
`StudyInstanceUID` is the prediction unit and `train_series.csv` contains series-level entries. Public participant pipelines iterate through DICOM series, sort slices, decode pixel data and construct per-series volumes before study-level aggregation. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn19search0\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
A detailed public competition pipeline reports that an examination can contain anything from a single series to more than a dozen and explicitly selects fluid-sensitive sequences and tries to cover sagittal, coronal and axial planes. That should be regarded as **participant-observed dataset behavior**, not an organizer-published protocol specification. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
This variability creates several modeling problems.\\\
\\\
First, \\\\\\\\(m_i\\\\\\\\), the number of series, changes between studies. A standard neural network expects a fixed tensor shape; a clinical MRI examination is naturally a **set of variable-length volumes**.\\\
\\\
Second, slice counts within a series vary. Models therefore need subsampling, pooling, padding, recurrent/set aggregation, or volumetric resampling.\\\
\\\
Third, MRI contrast depends strongly on acquisition sequence. T1-weighted, T2-weighted, proton-density and fluid-sensitive/fat-suppressed acquisitions emphasize different tissue characteristics. I found no authoritative released table giving the exact distribution of sequence categories in this competition, so any exact protocol taxonomy should be derived directly from DICOM metadata rather than assumed.\\\
\\\
Fourth, geometric resolution, slice spacing and orientation may differ between acquisitions and centers. Public participants inspect tags such as `SeriesDescription`, `ImageOrientationPatient`, `ImagePositionPatient`, `Manufacturer` and `MagneticFieldStrength`; their actual distributions require a local dataset audit. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn15search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
This is one reason I would avoid jumping immediately to a monolithic 3D CNN. A knee MRI study here is not necessarily one standardized isotropic volume.\\\
\\\
**Patient count and demographics.** I could not verify a reliable number of unique patients. `StudyInstanceUID` counts examinations, not necessarily unique individuals, and I found no authoritative statement proving that every study belongs to a different patient.\\\
\\\
That matters for research splitting: if repeated examinations of a patient exist, splitting by study rather than patient could leak patient-specific anatomy or acquisition patterns across folds.\\\
\\\
Demographic metadata is also ambiguous. The competition data description has apparently referred to `PatientSex`, yet public inspection reports a 14-column `train.csv` without that field; participants report that `PatientSex` is obtainable from at least some DICOM headers, including test DICOMs. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn15search0\\\\ue202turn16search8\\\\ue202turn16search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
I therefore would **not register a demographic-fairness project until the DICOM headers have been audited** for completeness of sex, age and patient identifiers.\\\
\\\
**Labels and Ground Truth**\\\
\\\
The 12 competition targets are binary study-level findings. There are no competition-provided bounding-box or pixel masks in the submission target.\\\
\\\
The most important fact is that **the clinical reports and the challenge expert labels were produced by different processes**.\\\
\\\
The original report is the report produced in normal clinical practice. The challenge reference labels were subsequently assigned through independent image review according to the challenge rubric. Competition-host responses state that annotated-reference studies were independently reviewed by **two subspecialty-trained musculoskeletal radiologists**, with disagreements adjudicated. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn36search2\\\\ue202turn17search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
RSNA separately acknowledges a large international group of musculoskeletal radiologist annotators recruited through the Society of Skeletal Radiology and International Skeletal Society and identifies MD.ai as the annotation tooling provider. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn44view2\\\\ue202turn44view3\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The hidden assessment dataset is described by RSNA as expert annotated. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
This gives the challenge a useful conceptual chain:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{MRI}\\\
\\\\\\\\xrightarrow{\\\\\\\\text{original clinical radiologist}}\\\
\\\\\\\\text{free-text report}\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
while independently,\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{MRI}\\\
\\\\\\\\xrightarrow{\\\\\\\\text{challenge MSK readers + adjudication}}\\\
\\\\\\\\text{challenge reference labels}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
Therefore,\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{report-derived label}\\\
\\\\\\\
eq\\\
\\\\\\\\text{challenge ground truth}\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
in general.\\\
\\\
That disagreement is not merely theoretical. Competition-host discussion explicitly explains that a source report and challenge label can differ because the challenge label comes from independent image review rather than simply transcribing the original report. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn17search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
This is arguably the single most important research property of the dataset.\\\
\\\
**The public 58-study expert subset.** Public notebooks and discussion indicate that only 58 of the 4,407 training studies carry official labels. The rest provide reports from which competitors are expected to derive useful supervision. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn34search0\\\\ue202turn30search17\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
This is not ordinary semi-supervised learning where unlabeled data contain no target information. It is closer to:\\\
\\\
- a tiny amount of **strong supervision**;\\\
- a large amount of **naturally weak supervision**;\\\
- with a structured relationship between the weak label source and the strong target.\\\
\\\
This makes the 58 studies potentially more valuable as a **calibration/audit set for weak-label generation** than simply as 58 extra training examples.\\\
\\\
A particularly sophisticated participant pipeline illustrates this: it used an open-weight LLM to infer the twelve labels from each report, validated and revised the extraction logic using the 58 expert studies, fused multiple pseudo-label sources, and then trained an image MIL model. Its reported report-label extractor obtained substantially imperfect agreement with the gold subset, and the author concluded that label quality was likely a larger bottleneck than image architecture. That is engineering evidence, not peer-reviewed evidence, but it is highly informative about the competition's practical structure. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Role of Radiology Reports**\\\
\\\
RSNA describes the reports as the **original radiology reports** and emphasizes that this is deliberately a more realistic setup in which diagnostic information is not provided in neatly structured tables. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
They should therefore be thought of as naturally occurring free text rather than a clean ontology.\\\
\\\
Reports can encode target information in several forms:\\\
\\\
\u003e direct positive finding \\\\u2192 \\\\u201cACL tear\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
\u003e explicit negative finding \\\\u2192 \\\\u201cACL intact\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
\u003e uncertain finding \\\\u2192 \\\\u201cpossible partial tear\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
\u003e indirect description \\\\u2192 wording implying inflammation without using \\\\u201csynovitis\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
\u003e severity-dependent description \\\\u2192 \\\\u201ctrace joint fluid\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
\u003e contextual or historical description \\\\u2192 previous surgery or chronic injury\\\
\\\
\u003e omission \\\\u2192 the structure may not be mentioned at all.\\\
\\\
The last category is especially important.\\\
\\\
A common competition shortcut is:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{not mentioned} \\\\\\\\Rightarrow 0.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
A serious participant's report-labeling pipeline explicitly made that assumption for its pseudo-label generation. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Scientifically, however,\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
P(Y=0\\\\\\\\mid \\\\\\\\text{not mentioned})\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
need not equal 1.\\\
\\\
Radiologists often prioritize clinically meaningful positives and may omit normal findings. Conversely, style and reporting conventions vary between institutions. Whether \\\\u201cunmentioned\\\\u201d should be interpreted as **negative** or **unlabeled** is therefore an excellent empirical research question.\\\
\\\
This connects directly to established weak-supervision literature. CheXpert, for example, automatically extracted 14 chest-radiograph observations from reports while explicitly representing uncertain labels rather than collapsing all report language into clean binary ground truth. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search3\\\\ue202turn38search7\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
CheXbert subsequently demonstrated that BERT-based report labeling combined with rule-derived and expert annotations can improve radiology-report labeling over purely rule-based extraction. Report-derived weak supervision is therefore established; what is unusual here is the **multilingual, musculoskeletal, MRI, multi-series, low-gold-label setting**.\\\
\\\
**Multilingual Aspect**\\\
\\\
RSNA says the dataset contains reports in approximately **a dozen languages**. It has not, in the official pages I could access, published the definitive list and per-language counts. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The site acknowledgments strongly suggest substantial linguistic diversity, but language should never be inferred mechanically from country: radiology departments may report in English, multiple local languages, or mixed language. Therefore language should be detected or taken from verified metadata, not assigned using institution country. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn44view0\\\\ue202turn44view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The field of multilingual radiology NLP is growing but is materially less standardized than English chest-radiograph NLP.\\\
\\\
PARROT, published around 2025\\\\u20132026, contains 2,658 radiology reports from 76 authors in 21 countries and 13 languages, but those reports were intentionally created as fictional reports for open multilingual NLP benchmarking and are not paired with the corresponding real diagnostic images. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn39search0\\\\ue202turn39search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
MOSAIC, presented at ClinicalNLP/LREC 2026, specifically addresses multilingual, taxonomy-agnostic radiology-report classification using a compact MedGemma-based approach suitable for relatively modest hardware. Its experiments cover multiple languages and illustrate that report classification can be adapted with comparatively small labeled datasets. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn39search2\\\\ue202turn39search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Other recent work has examined cross-lingual clinical/radiology NLP with limited annotations and LLM translation of CT/MRI reports, demonstrating both feasibility and language-dependent variation. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn39search3\\\\ue202turn39search10\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Consequently, \\\\u201ctranslate every report to English with an LLM\\\\u201d is **not itself a research contribution**.\\\
\\\
A defensible research question would instead be:\\\
\\\
\u003e Does translation change the *error structure* of weak labels relative to direct multilingual extraction, and does that error propagate differently into downstream MRI models?\\\
\\\
That is substantially stronger.\\\
\\\
**Dataset Complexity and Unique Characteristics**\\\
\\\
Relative to older public knee-MRI datasets, the most distinctive characteristics are not individually unprecedented. What is unusual is their joint occurrence.\\\
\\\
**Multiseries MRI is not new.** MRNet already used sagittal, coronal and axial knee MRI series. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn41search0\\\\ue202turn41search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Multilabel knee classification is not new.** Large recent systems classify nine or even more than twenty knee abnormalities. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search2\\\\ue202turn42search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Report-derived weak labels are not new.** CheXpert, CheXbert, NegBio and large report-labeled imaging studies predate this challenge. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search3\\\\ue202turn24search1\\\\ue202turn24academia28\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Image\\\\u2013report representation learning is not new.** GLoRIA, CheXzero and subsequent chest-X-ray foundation models have shown that radiology reports can provide useful natural supervision. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search1\\\\ue202turn38search6\\\\ue202turn38search20\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Multilingual radiology NLP is not entirely new.** PARROT and MOSAIC are direct counterexamples. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn39search1\\\\ue202turn39search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
What **is** unusual is:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\boxed{\\\
\\\\\\\\text{multicenter clinical MRI}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{variable multiseries studies}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{12 findings}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{multilingual reports}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{tiny expert subset}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{image-only inference}\\\
}\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
That specific combination creates research questions older knee datasets could not easily support.\\\
\\\
**Important Biases / Risks / Limitations**\\\
\\\
Several risks can be identified, but they must be separated into demonstrated issues and hypotheses.\\\
\\\
**Demonstrated or directly documented issues.**\\\
\\\
The dataset has extreme strong-label scarcity in the public training partition: approximately 58 expert-labeled studies versus thousands of report-supervised studies. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search17\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Report-derived labels can disagree with challenge labels because the report and challenge annotation arise from separate readings. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn17search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The reports are multilingual and the imaging originates from many international institutions, creating genuine heterogeneity. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn44view0\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The inference modality differs from the training information: reports are present in training but not the public test CSV. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn36search1\\\\ue202turn30search20\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The number of series per examination is variable according to public dataset inspection. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Likely risks that need empirical testing.**\\\
\\\
**Class imbalance** is likely because fracture, ligament abnormality, OA, effusion and cysts are not expected to have identical prevalences, but a reliable distribution of **expert** labels cannot be inferred from the report-generated labels. I would not quote pseudo-label prevalence as dataset ground truth.\\\
\\\
**Site/scanner/protocol bias** is a serious possibility because the dataset is multicenter and participants can inspect acquisition metadata. Multi-center medical imaging generally exhibits scanner- and protocol-related domain shift, and a 2024 systematic review identified generalization as an unresolved issue in medical-image classification. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn42search4\\\\ue202turn32search4\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Language/site confounding** is an especially interesting hypothesis. If particular institutions mostly use particular languages, language could act as a proxy for site, and site could correlate with prevalence, referral patterns or protocol. That could let a report labeler exploit institutional priors rather than only clinical semantics. This has **not** been demonstrated in this dataset.\\\
\\\
**Report-style shortcut learning** is similarly plausible: templated wording, section titles and habitual expressions may identify institutions. Again, this needs testing.\\\
\\\
**Demographic bias** cannot yet be responsibly assessed because demographic completeness has not been verified.\\\
\\\
**Patient leakage** is unresolved because the number of unique patients and repeat examinations could not be verified.\\\
\\\
**Pseudo-label leakage** is a methodological danger for FYP experiments. A model must not be tuned against a report-derived \\\\u201cvalidation target\\\\u201d generated by the same labeler used for training and then interpreted as independent validation.\\\
\\\
**Target leakage through reports** is subtler. Reports are legitimate training supervision in this challenge, but a held-out study's report must not enter an image model through preprocessing, contrastive pretraining, pseudo-label calibration or representation learning if that study is supposed to form an independent validation fold.\\\
\\\
These concerns are more scientifically valuable than maximizing the Kaggle score: they determine whether an apparent improvement reflects real MRI interpretation or merely easier exploitation of the data-generation process.\\\
\\\
## Comparison with prior datasets and the research landscape\\\
\\\
**Comparison With Previous Knee MRI Datasets**\\\
\\\
The natural historical comparator is **MRNet**.\\\
\\\
Bien et al.'s 2018 MRNet study used **1,370 knee MRI examinations** from Stanford and predicted three endpoints: overall abnormality, ACL tear and meniscal tear. The dataset was single-institution; labels were extracted from clinical reports, with a smaller expert-reviewed set used for validation. The model processed the standard imaging planes separately and combined their predictions. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn41search0\\\\ue202turn41search4\\\\ue202turn41search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
RSNA 2026 changes essentially every axis:\\\
\\\
- roughly three times as many public training studies before even considering hidden assessment data;\\\
- international multicenter acquisition;\\\
- 12 instead of three targets;\\\
- raw heterogeneous clinical DICOM series;\\\
- approximately a dozen report languages;\\\
- deliberately sparse public expert labels;\\\
- training-only text supervision;\\\
- a large hidden expert-annotated assessment set. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn30search14\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
A second useful comparator is **SKM-TEA**.\\\
\\\
Desai et al.'s Stanford Knee MRI Multi-Task Evaluation dataset contains 155 quantitative knee MRI scans, raw k-space data, scanner-generated DICOMs, tissue segmentations and bounding boxes for 16 pathologies. Its scientific purpose is largely end-to-end evaluation of reconstruction, quantitative MRI and image analysis rather than learning from naturally occurring reports. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn41search1\\\\ue202turn41search9\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
SKM-TEA is therefore richer in **dense spatial ground truth**, whereas RSNA 2026 is richer in **real-world multicenter and linguistic heterogeneity**.\\\
\\\
The literature has also moved well beyond MRNet in clinical scope.\\\
\\\
Astuto, Flament, Namiri et al. developed 3D CNN approaches to automatically identify and grade multiple knee abnormalities, showing that cartilage, bone marrow, meniscal and ligament-related analysis can already be performed jointly. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn21search2\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
A 2025 multicenter study by Xie et al. collected 12,825 patients at one training center plus external test cohorts and classified **nine knee abnormalities** using fat-suppressed proton-density MRI in three planes; its reference standard involved three musculoskeletal radiologists. DOI: `10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103534`. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search2\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
A 2026 dual-center study by Vuskov et al. studied **23 conditions** in 3,121 MRI examinations with an external test cohort and a 3D slice-transformer approach. DOI: `10.1007/s00330-025-12052-8`. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn42search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Therefore:\\\
\\\
\u003e **\\\\u201cPredict multiple knee abnormalities simultaneously\\\\u201d is not a sufficient FYP novelty claim in 2026.**\\\
\\\
**Relevant Research Landscape**\\\
\\\
**Knee MRI AI.** ACL tears, meniscal tears, cartilage lesions, osteoarthritis and associated findings have been studied extensively. A 2024 review specifically surveyed deep-learning MRI systems for assisted diagnosis of knee pathologies, while earlier systematic reviews already documented a rapidly expanding knee-imaging literature. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search3\\\\ue202turn40search12\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
ACL classification alone is particularly mature. A 2022 study trained an ACL-tear detector using nearly 20,000 knee MRI examinations and report-derived labels and validated it on external datasets, achieving an internal AUC around 0.94. This is important because it demonstrates that **NLP-derived MRI labels at large scale are themselves not novel**. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search11\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Meniscus and cartilage injury classification, segmentation and severity staging have likewise been repeatedly studied, including weakly supervised and externally validated approaches. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn42search14\\\\ue202turn40search13\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Multi-label / multi-condition learning.** Recent nine- and 23-condition systems make clear that the scientific opportunity here is not simply adding more classifier heads. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search2\\\\ue202turn42search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Explicit label dependencies remain less thoroughly explored in this exact weak-label setting, however. One possible question is whether correlations such as OA\\\\u2013effusion\\\\u2013synovitis or ligament injury\\\\u2013contusion patterns help when individual labels are noisy. That would have to be framed as **noise-robust dependency modeling**, not merely \\\\u201cuse a graph neural network.\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
**Three-dimensional, multi-series and multi-view MRI learning.** MRNet's architecture established the idea of processing slices and planes separately before combining study evidence. Modern methods extend this with 2.5D stacks, attention pooling, 3D convolution, transformers and MIL. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn41search0\\\\ue202turn40search6\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
What is less routine is handling a **variable clinical protocol as a set of heterogeneous series**. Recent knee systems often standardize to a known set of sequences; for example, the 2025 multicenter system uses defined PD-fat-suppressed planes and the 2026 23-condition study describes a fixed routine sequence set. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search2\\\\ue202turn42search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
That makes the RSNA dataset's variable series composition potentially valuable.\\\
\\\
**Multimodal radiology AI.** Medical vision-language learning is mature enough that the basic premise has been demonstrated convincingly.\\\
\\\
Huang, Shen, Lungren and Yeung's **GLoRIA** at ICCV 2021 learned both global and local image\\\\u2013report alignment for label-efficient recognition. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Tiu et al.'s **CheXzero** trained on 377,110 chest-X-ray/report pairs and demonstrated zero-shot pathology classification without manually labeled training examples. DOI: `10.1038/s41551-022-00936-9`. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search6\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Subsequent systems such as KAD and MaCo extended medical image\\\\u2013report pretraining and zero/few-shot disease recognition. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search10\\\\ue202turn38search20\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The crucial caveat is that this literature is overwhelmingly dominated by **2D chest radiographs**, where one or a few images correspond to a report. A knee MRI examination consisting of many slices and variable sequences creates a substantially different alignment problem.\\\
\\\
**Weak supervision from radiology reports.** CheXpert is the seminal precedent: Irvin et al. built a rule-based labeler for 14 radiographic observations and explicitly modeled uncertain mentions. Its 224,316 chest radiographs demonstrate the scalability of report-derived supervision. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search3\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
NegBio introduced dependency-pattern processing for negation and uncertainty, while CheXbert moved toward transformer-based report labeling and expert adaptation. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn24search1\\\\ue202turn24academia28\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Modern LLMs make extraction easier to implement, but not necessarily scientifically solved. A 2024 survey of label-noise learning in medical imaging emphasizes that noisy labels can materially reduce generalization and that noise-aware learning remains an active methodological area. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn42search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The RSNA dataset offers a particularly clean setting to ask:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{How should weak labels be modeled when their source is semantically related to, but not identical to, the strong target?}\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
**Multilingual radiology NLP.** PARROT, MOSAIC, multilingual report translation studies and cross-lingual clinical NLP show that this is an active field rather than untouched territory. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn39search1\\\\ue202turn39search8\\\\ue202turn39search10\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
What remains comparatively unusual is connecting those multilingual reports directly to real multicenter MRI exams and then testing whether language-processing choices affect an image model.\\\
\\\
**Semi-supervised and self-supervised medical imaging.** The rationale for SSL is well established. Models Genesis demonstrated that self-supervised pretraining on 3D medical images could outperform training from scratch and conventional 2D transfer approaches across multiple applications; its Medical Image Analysis version has DOI `10.1016/j.media.2020.101840`. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn41search2\\\\ue202turn41search6\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Xie, Zhang, Xia and Wu's **UniMiSS**, ECCV 2022, explicitly learned across 2D and 3D medical data. DOI: `10.1007/978-3-031-19803-8_33`. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn41search3\\\\ue202turn41search7\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
For this FYP, the more interesting question is consequently not \\\\u201cdoes SSL work?\\\\u201d but:\\\
\\\
\u003e When every supposedly \\\\u201cunlabeled\\\\u201d study already has a report, is image-only SSL, text-derived pseudo-supervision, image\\\\u2013text pretraining, or a combination most label-efficient?\\\
\\\
**Multi-center generalization.** Generalization across sites and scanners remains an unresolved problem in medical imaging. Reviews emphasize changes in scanner hardware, acquisition protocol and populations as important domain-shift mechanisms. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn42search4\\\\ue202turn32search4\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
RSNA's broad geographic coverage could make this highly interesting \\\\u2014 **provided a usable site identifier survives de-identification**. At present I cannot verify that it does. This is one of the first things to audit before committing to a cross-site FYP.\\\
\\\
**Model efficiency.** Efficiency is particularly meaningful for MRI because an examination contains far more pixel data than a single radiograph. Public competition pipelines already subsample series and slices specifically to fit the Kaggle time budget, and RSNA is awarding efficiency prizes. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue202turn37view1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
A research contribution here should evaluate an **accuracy\\\\u2013compute\\\\u2013robustness frontier**, not merely make inference faster for leaderboard convenience.\\\
\\\
**Explainability and reliability.** Knee-MRI work already includes attention and saliency-based analyses. For example, Namiri et al.'s 2020 Radiology: Artificial Intelligence ACL study compared 2D/3D networks and used localization/saliency analysis; DOI `10.1148/ryai.2020190207`. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search6\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Therefore, \\\\u201cadd Grad-CAM\\\\u201d is not a defensible research gap.\\\
\\\
More scientifically interesting reliability questions include calibration, selective prediction, failure detection, whether attention remains anatomically stable under missing sequences, or whether a model's uncertainty correlates with report\\\\u2013image disagreement.\\\
\\\
**Current Approaches to This Specific Competition**\\\
\\\
Because the competition is active, this section must be interpreted as a snapshot of engineering practice, not peer-reviewed evidence.\\\
\\\
Public competitors are already doing many of the superficially obvious things:\\\
\\\
- extracting pseudo-labels from reports using LLMs;\\\
- combining multiple LLM/report labelers;\\\
- multilingual rule-based or assertion-aware parsing;\\\
- translating or normalizing reports;\\\
- DINOv2-style visual features;\\\
- 2D and 2.5D CNNs;\\\
- plane-aware MIL;\\\
- selecting fluid-sensitive series;\\\
- selecting sagittal/coronal/axial series;\\\
- attention or max pooling over slices;\\\
- multimodal/report-teacher approaches;\\\
- domain-adaptive pretraining;\\\
- ensembling pseudo-label sources. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn12search9\\\\ue202turn12search12\\\\ue202turn12search20\\\\ue202turn12search4\\\\ue202turn12search0\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
A particularly detailed public pipeline uses an LLM-derived label set, multiple weak-label sources, a shared 2D image backbone, slice pooling and series-level aggregation. It also documents an important validation leakage bug involving the 58 gold studies \\\\u2014 a useful warning for FYP methodology. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Other participant notebooks explicitly claim substantial leaderboard gains from improved report-derived labels. Again, these are not controlled scientific experiments, but they reinforce the point that:\\\
\\\
\u003e **\\\\u201cUse an LLM to extract labels from the reports\\\\u201d is already a baseline, not a novel project.** \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn12search13\\\\ue202turn12search9\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Likewise, \\\\u201cuse DINOv2,\\\\u201d \\\\u201cuse a 2.5D CNN,\\\\u201d \\\\u201cuse three planes,\\\\u201d or \\\\u201censemble several models\\\\u201d would be weak FYP ideas by themselves.\\\
\\\
**What Has Already Been Solved**\\\
\\\
The literature and live competition together let us reject several candidate FYP formulations.\\\
\\\
**Simple ACL detection:** extensively studied; not novel. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search11\\\\ue202turn40search6\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Simple meniscal-tear classification:** extensively studied; not novel. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn42search14\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Generic OA classification:** a large literature already exists.\\\
\\\
**\\\\u201cPredict many knee abnormalities\\\\u201d:** recent systems already handle nine to 23 conditions. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn40search2\\\\ue202turn42search8\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Fixed-plane CNN fusion:** MRNet and many successors already do this. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn41search0\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Generic self-supervised learning:** well established in medical imaging. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn41search2\\\\ue202turn41search3\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Generic image\\\\u2013text contrastive learning:** well established in radiology, especially chest radiographs. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search1\\\\ue202turn38search6\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Generic report label extraction:** mature enough to have rule, BERT and LLM baselines. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search3\\\\ue202turn24academia28\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Plain saliency/Grad-CAM:** insufficient novelty.\\\
\\\
That is useful news, not bad news: it tells us exactly where the research contribution must reside.\\\

## Research gaps and candidate FYP directions\\\
\\\
**Open Research Questions**\\\
\\\
The following are evidence-derived gaps rather than generic architecture suggestions.\\\
\\\
**Weak-label semantics.** Existing competition pipelines often turn reports directly into binary labels, sometimes interpreting non-mention as negative. But the challenge reference standard comes from independent image review, so it is unclear whether a four-state interpretation \\\\u2014 positive, negative, uncertain, unmentioned \\\\u2014 gives better downstream supervision. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue202turn17search1\\\\ue202turn38search3\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Weak-label calibration with tiny strong supervision.** We know the 58 gold studies can reveal systematic labeler errors, but it is unknown how best to use them: prompt tuning, per-class calibration, label-noise transition estimation, probabilistic label fusion, downstream loss correction, or some combination.\\\
\\\
**Training-only text.** Medical VLP generally assumes image\\\\u2013report pairing is useful representation supervision, while competitors mostly reduce reports to pseudo-labels. It is unclear whether discarding the richer text is optimal when the report disappears at inference. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search1\\\\ue202turn38search6\\\\ue202turn36search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Multilingual extraction.** Translation, direct multilingual modeling and multilingual rule-based extraction may induce different errors. Existing multilingual radiology NLP proves feasibility but does not answer which route produces the most useful *weak labels for downstream knee MRI*. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn39search8\\\\ue202turn39search10\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Variable clinical protocol.** Existing knee systems often assume predefined series, while RSNA studies can contain a variable set. It is unclear whether selecting a few canonical series is more reliable than a learned set representation, particularly when a sequence is absent. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue202turn40search2\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Domain shift.** The dataset is strongly multicenter, but site-wise model behavior has not yet been scientifically characterized. This gap is actionable only if site/vendor identifiers can be obtained. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn37view0\\\\ue202turn42search4\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Calibration under noisy supervision.** The Kaggle metric is ROC AUC, which ignores probability calibration. An image system trained from uncertain report labels could rank patients well while producing unreliable probabilities. This question is clinically meaningful but difficult to validate with only 58 public gold studies.\\\
\\\
**Efficiency versus evidence coverage.** Public solutions select only some series/slices. It remains unclear how much diagnostic evidence is lost and whether adaptive selection disproportionately harms particular abnormalities. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Candidate FYP Directions**\\\
\\\
### Candidate: Noise-aware weak supervision from reports\\\
\\\
**Research question:** Should report-derived training labels be modeled as uncertain/noisy observations rather than binary ground truth?\\\
\\\
**Motivation and gap:** The source report and challenge expert label are distinct readings, yet common competition pipelines turn reports into hard 0/1 labels. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn17search1\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Hypothesis:** Separating positive, negative, uncertain and unmentioned report states, then producing calibrated soft targets, will improve agreement with expert labels and improve downstream image classification relative to hard pseudo-labels.\\\
\\\
**Methodology:** Develop several report extractors, cross-fit them on the 58 strong-label cases, estimate condition-specific reliability, aggregate probabilistically, and train the same image model under hard versus soft/noise-aware supervision.\\\
\\\
**Baseline:** hard LLM pseudo-labels with unmentioned=negative; simple rule/lexicon labeler; standard BCE.\\\
\\\
**Evaluation:** study-bootstrap confidence intervals for report-label F1/sensitivity/specificity; per-label AUROC/AUPRC for the MRI model; macro AUROC; Brier score/calibration; statistical comparison across label strategies.\\\
\\\
**Contribution type:** mainly **empirical + methodological weak-supervision novelty**.\\\
\\\
**Novelty:** **High** in this dataset context. Label-noise learning itself is not new, but this exact naturally occurring multilingual report \\\\u2192 independent image-reference relationship is unusual. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn42search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Difficulty:** Moderate.\\\
\\\
**Compute:** free Kaggle GPU or one consumer GPU should be sufficient if using a 2D/2.5D MIL image baseline and a compact local text model.\\\
\\\
**FYP feasibility:** Excellent.\\\
\\\
**Publication potential:** **Strong** if the gold-label evaluation is carefully cross-fitted and the error taxonomy is rigorous.\\\
\\\
**Main risk:** 58 studies are small; per-class confidence intervals may be wide.\\\
\\\
### Candidate: Positive\\\\u2013unlabeled treatment of report non-mentions\\\
\\\
**Research question:** Is \\\\u201cnot mentioned in the report\\\\u201d better modeled as negative or unlabeled?\\\
\\\
**Gap:** Public report extractors often use non-mention as 0; radiology-report supervision historically distinguishes uncertainty and explicit negatives. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue202turn38search3\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Hypothesis:** Treating unmentioned findings using positive\\\\u2013unlabeled or partial-label losses will improve recall and reduce systematic false-negative pseudo-labeling.\\\
\\\
**Methodology:** Extract four semantic report states; compare closed-world binary targets, masked-loss targets and PU-learning objectives.\\\
\\\
**Baseline:** unmentioned=negative.\\\
\\\
**Evaluation:** expert-label agreement plus downstream MRI AUROC/AUPRC, particularly sensitivity for labels frequently omitted in reports.\\\
\\\
**Contribution:** clean **empirical weak-supervision finding**.\\\
\\\
**Novelty:** Moderate\\\\u2013High.\\\
\\\
**Difficulty:** Moderate.\\\
\\\
**Compute:** Kaggle/Colab/consumer GPU.\\\
\\\
**FYP feasibility:** Excellent.\\\
\\\
**Publication potential:** Reasonable to strong.\\\
\\\
**Main risk:** challenge rubric may make some report omissions genuinely uninformative, producing only modest gains.\\\
\\\
### Candidate: Reports as privileged information instead of pseudo-labels\\\
\\\
**Research question:** Are reports best converted into labels, or retained as semantic training information even though they disappear at inference?\\\
\\\
**Gap:** Medical VLP shows strong image\\\\u2013text learning, while competition solutions often collapse rich reports into 12 labels. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search1\\\\ue202turn38search6\\\\ue202turn12search9\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Hypothesis:** A multimodal teacher or image\\\\u2013text pretraining objective will transfer information not captured by the twelve pseudo-labels and produce a better image-only student.\\\
\\\
**Methodology:** Compare hard pseudo-label BCE, image\\\\u2013report contrastive pretraining, multimodal teacher\\\\u2192image student distillation, and a hybrid.\\\
\\\
**Baseline:** same image backbone trained purely from report-derived pseudo-labels.\\\
\\\
**Evaluation:** macro/per-label AUROC, AUPRC, label-efficiency curves, representation retrieval and robustness.\\\
\\\
**Contribution:** **multimodal methodological + empirical novelty**.\\\
\\\
**Novelty:** High.\\\
\\\
**Difficulty:** High.\\\
\\\
**Compute:** one 16\\\\u201324 GB consumer GPU is realistic with cached slice/series embeddings; free Kaggle GPUs possible with careful design.\\\
\\\
**FYP feasibility:** Good if tightly scoped.\\\
\\\
**Publication potential:** Strong.\\\
\\\
**Risk:** easy to overcomplicate; benefits may disappear once pseudo-labels are already high quality.\\\
\\\
### Candidate: Direct multilingual extraction versus translation to English\\\
\\\
**Research question:** Which strategy preserves knee-abnormality semantics best: direct multilingual modeling or translation followed by English extraction?\\\
\\\
**Gap:** multilingual radiology NLP and LLM translation are feasible, but their downstream weak-supervision consequences are underexplored. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn39search8\\\\ue202turn39search10\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Hypothesis:** Translation works well for straightforward findings but creates systematic assertion/severity errors for some abnormalities and languages.\\\
\\\
**Methodology:** compare direct multilingual encoder/LLM, machine translation + identical English labeler, and multilingual assertion rules.\\\
\\\
**Baseline:** translate-all-to-English pipeline.\\\
\\\
**Evaluation:** gold-label agreement overall and by language, label and linguistic phenomenon; downstream MRI performance.\\\
\\\
**Contribution:** **empirical multilingual NLP novelty**.\\\

### Proposed title: *When the Report Is Not the Ground Truth: Noise-Aware Multilingual Weak Supervision for Multi-Label Knee MRI*\\\
\\\
**Problem statement**\\\
\\\
The overwhelming majority of public RSNA training studies have a radiology report but no direct expert challenge label. The report and expert challenge target represent different readings of the MRI, meaning report-derived pseudo-labels are noisy observations rather than ground truth. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn17search1\\\\ue202turn30search17\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Current competition practice often produces hard binary report labels. The fundamental research problem is whether explicitly modeling the **semantic and statistical uncertainty of report-derived supervision** produces more reliable MRI models.\\\
\\\
**Primary research question**\\\
\\\
\u003e Does noise-aware probabilistic supervision derived from multilingual radiology reports outperform hard report-derived pseudo-labels for multi-label knee MRI classification?\\\
\\\
Useful secondary questions are:\\\
\\\
Does treating non-mention as unlabeled outperform treating it as negative?\\\
\\\
Which of the 12 findings exhibit the greatest report/reference mismatch?\\\
\\\
Do translation and direct multilingual extraction produce different error structures?\\\
\\\
Does report-label confidence predict downstream image-model error?\\\
\\\
**Hypothesis**\\\
\\\
The main hypothesis is:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{four-state report extraction}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{condition-specific reliability calibration}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{soft/noise-aware labels}\\\
\u003e\\\
\\\\\\\\text{hard binary pseudo-labels}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
I would specifically expect the gain to be largest for abnormalities where wording, severity thresholds or indirect descriptions are heterogeneous.\\\
\\\
**Research contribution**\\\
\\\
The strongest contribution would not be \\\\u201ca new knee classifier.\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
It would be:\\\
\\\
\u003e A controlled characterization of how multilingual clinical reports differ from independent expert image-reference labels, together with an experimentally validated weak-supervision strategy for exploiting that mismatch.\\\
\\\
That is a much stronger thesis claim.\\\
\\\
**Dataset usage**\\\
\\\
Use all public training reports for weak supervision.\\\
\\\
Use corresponding MRI examinations for downstream image learning.\\\
\\\
Use the 58 officially labeled studies as the strong-label calibration/evaluation pool. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search17\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Do not use hidden test reports because there are none in the operational Kaggle setup. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn36search1\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Methodology**\\\
\\\
First, identify report language using a deterministic language detector and manually inspect a small sample for obvious errors.\\\
\\\
Second, produce report states for each of the 12 findings:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
z_{ik}\\\\\\\\in\\\
\\\\\\\\{\\\\\\\\text{positive},\\\
\\\\\\\\text{negative},\\\
\\\\\\\\text{uncertain},\\\
\\\\\\\\text{unmentioned}\\\\\\\\}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
Implement at least three independent weak-label sources:\\\
\\\
a simple terminology/assertion baseline;\\\
\\\
a translation-to-English + fixed extractor baseline;\\\
\\\
a direct multilingual model/LLM baseline.\\\
\\\
Do not design prompts on all 58 gold examples and then evaluate on those same 58. That would simply overfit the label extractor.\\\
\\\
Instead use **cross-fitting**. For example, partition the gold studies into five folds. For fold \\\\\\\\(q\\\\\\\\), calibrate rules/prompts/reliability using only the other four folds and evaluate the held-out fold. Repeat until every gold study has an out-of-fold report prediction.\\\
\\\
Because \\\\\\\\(n=58\\\\\\\\) is tiny, report study-level bootstrap 95% confidence intervals rather than pretending the 696 individual label slots are independent.\\\
\\\
Next estimate label-specific reliability parameters. A simple version could estimate\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
P(Y_k=1 \\\\\\\\mid z_k=\\\\\\\\text{positive})\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
and analogous probabilities for the four report states.\\\
\\\
A richer version could combine multiple labelers:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
P(Y_k=1\\\\\\\\mid z^{(1)}_k,z^{(2)}_k,z^{(3)}_k).\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
These become soft supervision targets.\\\
\\\
Train one fixed image architecture under several conditions:\\\
\\\
hard pseudo-labels;\\\
\\\
hard labels with uncertain/unmentioned masked;\\\
\\\
probabilistic soft labels;\\\
\\\
probabilistic labels + the 58 strong labels;\\\
\\\
optionally a robust/noisy-label loss.\\\
\\\
Keep the image architecture deliberately boring enough that label strategy, not backbone engineering, is the independent variable. A 2.5D EfficientNet/ConvNeXt or similar MIL backbone is adequate.\\\
\\\
**Baselines**\\\
\\\
Rule-based extraction.\\\
\\\
Direct LLM binary extraction.\\\
\\\
Hard pseudo-label BCE.\\\
\\\
Unmentioned=negative.\\\
\\\
Expert-58-only image training, even though it will perform poorly, as a useful lower-data reference.\\\
\\\
Potentially a CheXpert-style uncertain-label policy adapted to the knee ontology. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search3\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Ablations**\\\
\\\
Remove explicit uncertainty.\\\
\\\
Collapse unmentioned into negative.\\\
\\\
Remove language-specific processing.\\\
\\\
Remove per-class calibration.\\\
\\\
Use only one weak labeler versus multiple.\\\
\\\
Replace soft labels with thresholded hard labels.\\\
\\\
Remove the 58 strong examples from image training after using them only for label calibration.\\\
\\\
These ablations would tell you **why** any improvement occurs.\\\
\\\
**Evaluation metrics**\\\
\\\
For report extraction:\\\
\\\
macro F1;\\\
\\\
per-label sensitivity/specificity/PPV;\\\
\\\
balanced accuracy;\\\
\\\
agreement with gold;\\\
\\\
study-bootstrap confidence intervals.\\\
\\\
For MRI:\\\
\\\
macro ROC AUC to maintain competition comparability;\\\
\\\
per-label ROC AUC;\\\
\\\
AUPRC because class imbalance may make ROC AUC insufficient;\\\
\\\
Brier score;\\\
\\\
ECE if sample size permits;\\\
\\\
bootstrap intervals across studies.\\\
\\\
Leaderboard score should be secondary evidence only.\\\
\\\
**Experimental splits and leakage prevention**\\\
\\\
The split unit should be the **patient**, if PatientID can be reliably recovered. If not, use StudyInstanceUID while explicitly acknowledging that repeat-patient leakage could not be excluded.\\\
\\\
Gold cases used to evaluate the report labeler must have out-of-fold prompts/calibration.\\\
\\\
A gold validation study's report must not be used to generate an image-training label.\\\
\\\
If any representation pretraining is performed, the entire held-out study \\\\u2014 MRI and report \\\\u2014 should be excluded from that fold's training pipeline.\\\
\\\
If site metadata become available, report both random and site-aware results.\\\
\\\
**Expected outcomes**\\\
\\\
The hypothesis is supported if probabilistic/noise-aware pseudo-labels show reliably better expert-label agreement and downstream image performance than hard extraction.\\\
\\\
Even a finding such as:\\\
\\\
\u003e \\\\u201cSoft targets do not improve overall AUROC, but treating non-mention as unlabeled substantially improves ACL/MCL sensitivity while hurting effusion precision\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
would constitute meaningful research.\\\
\\\
**Minimum viable contribution**\\\
\\\
A systematic, cross-fitted study of report/reference disagreement for the 12 findings is itself a respectable FYP contribution even if the downstream MRI model does not improve.\\\
\\\
You could produce:\\\
\\\
a taxonomy of report-label errors;\\\
\\\
condition-specific weak-label reliability estimates;\\\
\\\
multilingual extraction comparisons;\\\
\\\
recommendations for using reports as supervision.\\\
\\\
**Risks and fallback scope**\\\
\\\
The main risk is \\\\\\\\(n=58\\\\\\\\).\\\
\\\
Fallback: make the primary contribution a **weak-label methodology and audit**, and keep the MRI model as a downstream validation rather than attempting a SOTA imaging system.\\\
\\\
This would actually strengthen the thesis focus.\\\
\\\
### Proposed title: *Reports as Privileged Information: Multimodal Supervision for Knee MRI with Image-Only Inference*\\\
\\\
**Problem statement**\\\
\\\
The dataset contains paired image/report data during training, but reports disappear at inference. Most simple approaches discard nearly all report information by converting each report into twelve binary numbers. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn36search1\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Medical vision-language work shows that full reports can teach image representations beyond explicit labels. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search1\\\\ue202turn38search6\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
The research question is therefore whether reports should be treated as:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{label generators}\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
or as\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{privileged semantic supervision}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
**Primary research question**\\\
\\\
\u003e When reports are available only during training, does retaining their semantic representation through image\\\\u2013text learning or knowledge distillation outperform converting them directly into pseudo-labels?\\\
\\\
Secondary questions:\\\
\\\
Does contrastive pretraining improve label efficiency?\\\
\\\
Does a multimodal teacher help abnormalities poorly extracted by text rules?\\\
\\\
Does multimodal learning remain beneficial once pseudo-label quality is already high?\\\
\\\
**Hypothesis**\\\
\\\
A hybrid strategy will perform best:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{image\\\\u2013report representation learning}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{noise-aware label supervision}\\\
\\\\\\\\rightarrow\\\
\\\\\\\\text{image-only student}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
**Research contribution**\\\
\\\
The novelty is not a new generic VLM.\\\
\\\
It is a systematic answer to **how training-only clinical text should be used in a multi-series MRI classification problem**.\\\
\\\
This is a form of **learning using privileged information**.\\\
\\\
**Dataset usage**\\\
\\\
Use all 4,407 public image\\\\u2013report training pairs based on current public-file observations. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search14\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
Use the 58 expert studies for strict evaluation/calibration.\\\
\\\
At inference and validation, the final student receives only MRI.\\\
\\\
**Methodology**\\\
\\\
Construct one image encoder that creates a study representation:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
v_i=f_\\\\\\\\theta(X_i).\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
Construct one multilingual text encoder:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
t_i=g_\\\\\\\\phi(R_i).\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
Compare four regimes.\\\
\\\
**Pseudo-label baseline**\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
R_i \\\\\\\\rightarrow \\\\\\\\tilde Y_i;\\\
\\\\\\\\qquad\\\
f_\\\\\\\\theta(X_i)\\\\\\\\rightarrow \\\\\\\\tilde Y_i.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
**Image\\\\u2013text pretraining**\\\
\\\
Learn \\\\\\\\(v_i\\\\\\\\) and \\\\\\\\(t_i\\\\\\\\) with contrastive alignment, then discard the text encoder and fine-tune the image encoder.\\\
\\\
**Multimodal teacher**\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
h(X_i,R_i)\\\\\\\\rightarrow Y_i\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
or weak targets; distill its logits/features into\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
f(X_i).\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
**Hybrid**\\\
\\\
Combine contrastive representation learning with the strongest noise-aware labels from proposal one.\\\
\\\
To keep this undergraduate-feasible, precompute text embeddings and preferably slice/series image embeddings rather than training a massive multimodal transformer end-to-end.\\\
\\\
**Baselines**\\\
\\\
ImageNet-pretrained image model.\\\
\\\
Image-only self-supervised model.\\\
\\\
Hard pseudo-label supervised model.\\\
\\\
GLoRIA/CheXzero-style global contrastive baseline adapted conceptually to study-level MRI. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn38search1\\\\ue202turn38search6\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
**Ablation studies**\\\
\\\
Full report versus Impression/Conclusion when reliably parsable.\\\
\\\
Original multilingual text versus translation.\\\
\\\
Contrastive objective without pseudo-labels.\\\
\\\
Pseudo-label objective without contrastive learning.\\\
\\\
Logit distillation versus feature distillation.\\\
\\\
Freeze versus fine-tune text encoder.\\\
\\\
Different amounts of strong-label supervision.\\\
\\\
**Evaluation metrics**\\\
\\\
Macro/per-label AUROC and AUPRC.\\\
\\\
Label-efficiency curves.\\\
\\\
Brier/calibration metrics.\\\
\\\
Image\\\\u2013report retrieval as an auxiliary representation metric.\\\
\\\
Potential robustness tests under missing series.\\\
\\\
**Experimental splits**\\\
\\\
The strict rule is:\\\
\\\
\u003e A validation study's report must never participate in training, including contrastive pretraining.\\\
\\\
This is easy to violate accidentally because image\\\\u2013text pretraining is sometimes called \\\\u201cself-supervised.\\\\u201d It is still training on that patient's information.\\\
\\\
Use patient-grouped folds if patient IDs are available.\\\
\\\
Cross-fit all choices involving the 58 gold cases.\\\
\\\
**Expected outcomes**\\\
\\\
Support for the hypothesis would be a consistent image-only improvement after report-based representation learning or distillation.\\\
\\\
A scientifically interesting rejection would be:\\\
\\\
\u003e \\\\u201cOnce report labels are carefully calibrated, preserving full report semantics offers no additional benefit.\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
That would still answer a valuable question.\\\
\\\
**Minimum viable contribution**\\\
\\\
A controlled comparison of four supervision paradigms under the same image backbone and compute budget is sufficient for a strong empirical thesis even without a novel architecture.\\\
\\\
**Risks and fallback scope**\\\
\\\
Multimodal systems are easy to make too complex.\\\
\\\
Fallback:\\\
\\\
compare only\\\
\\\
hard pseudo-labeling,\\\
\\\
image\\\\u2013text contrastive pretraining,\\\
\\\
and hybrid training\\\
\\\
with fixed pretrained encoders.\\\
\\\
That is enough.\\\

### Proposed title: *Protocol-Robust Multi-Series Learning for Real-World Knee MRI Under Missing and Variable Acquisitions*\\\
\\\
**Problem statement**\\\
\\\
Research datasets often standardize MRI inputs. The RSNA studies instead contain variable numbers and types of clinical series; public competitors routinely select only a few preferred acquisitions. \\\\ue200cite\\\\ue202turn30search5\\\\ue201\\\
\\\
A fixed \\\\u201csagittal + coronal + axial\\\\u201d architecture makes a hidden assumption:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\forall i,\\\\\\\\quad X_i \\\\\\\\approx\\\
\\\\\\\\{S_{\\\\\\\\mathrm{sag}},S_{\\\\\\\\mathrm{cor}},S_{\\\\\\\\mathrm{ax}}\\\\\\\\}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
That assumption is unlikely to hold perfectly in heterogeneous real clinical data.\\\
\\\
**Primary research question**\\\
\\\
\u003e Does representing a knee MRI examination as a typed variable set of series improve robustness to missing and heterogeneous protocols compared with fixed canonical series selection?\\\
\\\
Secondary questions:\\\
\\\
Which abnormalities depend most on specific planes/sequences?\\\
\\\
How gracefully does performance degrade as evidence is removed?\\\
\\\
Does modality/series dropout during training improve robustness?\\\
\\\
Does using every available series help or simply introduce irrelevant information?\\\
\\\
**Hypothesis**\\\
\\\
A set model with series-type embeddings and deliberate series dropout will outperform fixed-series models under missing-series/protocol perturbations, even if standard IID AUROC is similar.\\\
\\\
That last clause is important. The research contribution is **robustness**, not necessarily higher ordinary validation AUC.\\\
\\\
**Dataset usage**\\\
\\\
Use DICOM series metadata to identify:\\\
\\\
orientation/plane;\\\
\\\
sequence descriptors;\\\
\\\
fluid-sensitive characteristics where reliable;\\\
\\\
series dimensions and slice count.\\\
\\\
Encode each series separately, then aggregate over the study.\\\
\\\
**Methodology**\\\
\\\
Stage one: create a robust series taxonomy from DICOM tags and geometry rather than only free-text `SeriesDescription`.\\\
\\\
Stage two: build a shared series encoder.\\\
\\\
For example:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
e_{ij}\\\
=\\\
f_{\\\\\\\\theta}(S_{ij}).\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
Stage three: augment it with series metadata:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\hat e_{ij}\\\
=\\\
e_{ij}\\\
+\\\
p_{\\\\\\\\mathrm{plane}}\\\
+\\\
p_{\\\\\\\\mathrm{sequence}}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
Stage four: aggregate the variable set:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
z_i =\\\
A(\\\\\\\\{\\\\\\\\hat e_{i1},...,\\\\\\\\hat e_{im_i}\\\\\\\\})\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
using mean pooling, attention pooling or a small Set Transformer.\\\
\\\
Stage five: predict all 12 labels from \\\\\\\\(z_i\\\\\\\\).\\\
\\\
During training, randomly remove some series:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
X'_i \\\\\\\\subset X_i\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
to force missing-modality robustness.\\\
\\\
**Baselines**\\\
\\\
Best single series.\\\
\\\
Fixed sagittal/coronal/axial selection.\\\
\\\
Three-series mean/logit fusion.\\\
\\\
All-series simple average.\\\
\\\
All-series attention without metadata.\\\
\\\
Typed-set model without series dropout.\\\
\\\
**Ablation studies**\\\
\\\
Remove plane embeddings.\\\
\\\
Remove sequence embeddings.\\\
\\\
Remove series dropout.\\\
\\\
Random versus informed series selection.\\\
\\\
Different maximum numbers of series.\\\
\\\
Different slice budgets per series.\\\
\\\
Attention versus simple mean pooling.\\\
\\\
**Evaluation metrics**\\\
\\\
Standard macro/per-label AUROC.\\\
\\\
AUPRC.\\\
\\\
Performance after removing one preferred series.\\\
\\\
Performance after keeping only one or two planes.\\\
\\\
Performance versus fraction of series removed.\\\
\\\
Worst-case drop:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\Delta_k =\\\
AUC_k^{full}-AUC_k^{missing}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
Compute/memory/runtime versus performance.\\\
\\\
**Experimental splits**\\\
\\\
Keep every series from one patient/study in exactly one fold.\\\
\\\
Do not split series independently.\\\
\\\
If patient identifiers exist, group by patient.\\\
\\\
If site/vendor can be extracted, add leave-domain-out experiments as a secondary analysis.\\\
\\\
Use the same report-derived labels across architectural comparisons so weak-label quality is not an uncontrolled variable.\\\
\\\
**Expected outcomes**\\\
\\\
Support for the hypothesis would be similar or better IID performance but materially smaller degradation when series are missing.\\\
\\\
For example, a result like\\\
\\\
\u003e fixed three-series model: \\\\u22120.08 macro AUC after removing one preferred series  \\\
\u003e typed-set + series-dropout model: \\\\u22120.025\\\
\\\
would be scientifically valuable even if their full-study AUCs were equal.\\\
\\\
**Minimum viable contribution**\\\
\\\
A rigorous benchmark quantifying **which series and planes contribute to each abnormality and how models fail under missing acquisitions** would itself be a useful empirical thesis.\\\
\\\
**Risks and fallback scope**\\\
\\\
The biggest risk is DICOM sequence normalization.\\\
\\\
Fallback:\\\
\\\
ignore fine-grained sequence identity and model only robustly inferred anatomical plane plus arbitrary set aggregation.\\\
\\\
That remains interesting and substantially reduces engineering scope.\\\
\\\
**Which Project I Recommend and Why**\\\
\\\
I recommend **Proposal One: noise-aware multilingual weak supervision**.\\\
\\\
The reason is not that it is the most technically impressive.\\\
\\\
It is that it has the best research structure:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\text{clear observed problem}\\\
\\\\\\\\rightarrow\\\
\\\\\\\\text{testable hypothesis}\\\
\\\\\\\\rightarrow\\\
\\\\\\\\text{strong baselines}\\\
\\\\\\\\rightarrow\\\
\\\\\\\\text{interpretable experiment}\\\
\\\\\\\\rightarrow\\\
\\\\\\\\text{useful outcome even when hypothesis fails}.\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
It directly targets what makes RSNA 2026 different.\\\
\\\
It also prevents the FYP from turning into months of DICOM preprocessing followed by backbone experimentation.\\\
\\\
The project can begin as a text/weak-label study, establish reliable pseudo-labels and only then add one deliberately standardized imaging model.\\\
\\\
A sensible registration-level formulation would be:\\\
\\\
\u003e **Investigating noise-aware weak supervision from multilingual radiology reports for multi-label knee MRI classification.**\\\
\\\
That wording is broad enough to survive changes in your eventual model but precise enough to constitute a research problem.\\\
\\\
Proposal Two would be my choice if you specifically want a more multimodal/deep-learning-heavy FYP.\\\
\\\
Proposal Three would be my choice if your strongest interest is computer vision/MRI rather than NLP.\\\

**Final Decision**\\\
\\\
### Is the RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection dataset a strong foundation for the FYP?\\\
\\\
**Yes \\\\u2014 strongly so.**\\\
\\\
But the reason is **not**:\\\
\\\
\u003e \\\\u201cIt is a new Kaggle competition with knee MRIs.\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
Nor is it:\\\
\\\
\u003e \\\\u201cI can try EfficientNet versus ViT.\\\\u201d\\\
\\\
The real research value is that the dataset exposes a realistic and difficult supervision structure:\\\
\\\
\\\\\\\\[\\\
\\\\\\\\boxed{\\\
\\\\\\\\text{thousands of heterogeneous MRI exams}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{multilingual clinical reports}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{very sparse expert training labels}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{independent expert reference standard}\\\
+\\\
\\\\\\\\text{image-only deployment}\\\
}\\\
\\\\\\\\]\\\
\\\
That structure supports questions that are both academically defensible and feasible for an undergraduate researcher.\\\
\\\
The **strongest problem to investigate before registration** is:\\\
\\\
\u003e **How should multilingual radiology reports be used as noisy weak supervision when the ultimate target is an independent expert interpretation of the MRI?**\\\
\\\
The second strongest is:\\\
\\\
\u003e **When text is available during training but not inference, is converting the report into pseudo-labels actually the optimal way to use it, or does image\\\\u2013text representation learning / knowledge distillation preserve useful information that binary labels discard?**\\\
\\\
The strongest imaging-centered alternative is:\\\
\\\
\u003e **How should models aggregate heterogeneous, variable MRI series, and can explicit missing-series robustness generalize better than selecting a fixed set of canonical planes?**\\\
\\\
Of those, the first has the most favorable FYP trade-off.\\\
\\\
It is **novel enough without requiring a novel architecture, experimentally testable, computationally realistic, directly motivated by the actual data-generation process, connected to mature literature for baselines, and capable of producing a valuable thesis even if the proposed method does not outperform the best Kaggle model.**\\\
\\\
That is exactly the kind of problem I would prefer an undergraduate research student to register over a vaguely defined \\\\u201cdeep learning for knee abnormality detection\\\\u201d project.\\\
